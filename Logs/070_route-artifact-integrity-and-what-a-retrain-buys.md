# All four generators — can we actually tell a chemist how to make what we selected?

**Date:** 2026-08-24, ~2pm

## Question

Our libraries are chosen for how cheaply they can be made — so for every molecule we select, do we
still hold the recipe that says how to make it?

## Context & Summary

**Context** — Everything this benchmark claims rests on a cost: a library is good if it needs few
reactions. Two separate uses depend on holding the actual recipe rather than just its length. The
competitor comparison prices our molecules against an optimizer, which needs to see the shared
intermediates; and the dataset we intend to publish is a *synthesis guide* — the whole point is that a
chemist can read it and order the building blocks. Entry `062` had already tripped over a version of
this (an arm that priced the wrong molecules and still reported success), so this entry asks the
question directly and across every cell rather than one at a time.

**Summary** — We audit all 36 (generator × target × seed) cells for the three things a usable recipe
needs, ask for each missing piece whether it can be recovered and at what cost, and measure the one
case where the answer was genuinely unknown. We then fix the checks so the same gap cannot open again
silently, and write down exactly what a re-training run would have to cover.

## Answer

**Most of our recipes were never written down, and most of what is missing cannot be recovered at any
price — but the results we have published do not depend on the missing part.** Only **5 of 36** cells
hold a complete recipe today. Of the rest, 23 are missing a piece that can only be regenerated by
producing a *different* library, and 6 are missing one that only exists during training. Nothing is
repairable by re-running the cheap stage, which is what we had assumed for two days.

The headline results survive because they never used the recipes: the reaction *counts* they rely on
come from a separate accounting that was always complete. What the gap costs us is the breadth of the
route dataset and one supporting ablation — not the main claim.

The one measurement that decided the plan: we had hoped a fresh draw from a frozen model would
reproduce the molecules it drew before, which would have made recovery cheap. It does not, for a
reason nobody had checked — the draw depends on a hidden per-process setting that was never recorded.
Two runs of the same model with the same random seed agree on only **61%** of their scaffolds. So the
old libraries cannot be re-derived; they can only be replaced.

## Relevance to our Publication

This entry is what lets us describe the dataset accurately instead of optimistically. We can publish
routes for four cells and say so, rather than promising coverage we cannot deliver and discovering the
hole in review. It also converts three near-misses into a stated standard: every one of the failures
here produced a confident, plausible, *wrong* answer rather than an error, which is exactly the class
of problem a reviewer cannot check for us. Saying "we audited this and here is what we hold" is worth
more than a larger dataset with unstated gaps.

It also settles the scope of one ablation honestly. The supporting arm that hands our own molecules to
the competitor's optimizer cannot be quoted as a ratio — the optimizer does not finish on libraries the
size ours produces. Reporting that as a bound, and noting our own selection answers the same problem in
about a second, is a stronger claim than a number we would have to caveat anyway.

### Method

1. Audit all 36 cells for: a populated `routes.json` (sample stage), `children[].reaction`
   (enumerate stage), and expandable fragment recipes (train stage).
2. For each missing piece, identify which stage produces it and therefore what re-running costs.
3. Probe the one open question — whether a fresh sample reproduces the original — with two fresh
   samples differing only in `PYTHONHASHSEED`.
4. Re-run the competitor arm on the corrected (larger) enumerations at a 12 h solver cap and a relaxed
   optimality tolerance, to see whether it converges.

```
python experiments/lsd_hubs/matrix16/check_route_readiness.py

    experiments/lsd_hubs/campaign/submit_hb_enum_sb.sh              # jobs 74633/74634
```

### Results

**1 — what we hold, across all 36 cells.**

| | cells | blocking stage |
|---|---|---|
| complete (routes + reactions + recipes) | **5** | — |
| `routes.json` empty | 23 | SAMPLE |
| recipes absent | 6 | TRAIN |
| not applicable (FragGFN: attachments, not reactions) | 12 | — |

The 5 complete cells are `scent_{seh,drd2}` at seeds 43/44, plus `scent_6td3` seed 43 (whose target is
parked on gate calibration). **Seed 42 — the primary seed — is absent from every one of them.**

**2 — the recipe gap is a logging window, not a property of any target.** Coverage by cell, where ❌ is
"no recipes":

| | s42 | s43 | s44 |
|---|---|---|---|
| scent_seh | ❌ | ✅ 1,600 | ✅ 1,600 |
| scent_drd2 | ❌ | ✅ 800 | ✅ 1,600 |
| scent_6td3 | ❌ | ✅ 400 | ✅ 400 |
| scent_clpp | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |

Recipe logging landed 2026-07-10, regressed for runs launched 07-14→07-26 (SLURM snapshots a submit
script at submit time, so an already-queued chain kept the old default), and has been on since the
default flipped in the runtime-resolved `.py` on 07-29. Every ❌ above falls inside that window. It is
**unrecoverable**: a fragment's route is observable only while it is being built; once promoted, the
model consumes it atomically and it never reappears as a reaction product.

**3 — the sample gap cannot be repaired either, and this was the measurement that decided it.**
`rxnflow_seh`, same checkpoint, same `--seed 42`, only `PYTHONHASHSEED` differing:

| | hs=0 | hs=12345 |
|---|---|---|
| records | 1,896 | 1,929 |
| identical order | no | |
| identical molecule set | no (overlap 976) | |
| **identical hub set** | **no — 994 of 1,619, 61%** | |

Per-process hash order feeds action-space construction, so an identical RNG draw over a
differently-ordered action list picks a different action. The originals ran with `PYTHONHASHSEED`
**unset**, i.e. a random value nobody recorded. RGFN was already known to behave this way; this shows
RxnFlow does too, which removes the last candidate for cheap recovery. A re-sample therefore
**replaces** a cell (new hubs ⇒ re-enumerate ⇒ re-campaign ⇒ its published numbers move) rather than
repairing it. Going forward `submit_cell.sh` exports `PYTHONHASHSEED=0`, so new samples are
reproducible; only the existing artifacts are one-of-a-kind, which makes the backups load-bearing.

**4 — the competitor's optimizer does not converge on the corrected pools.** HB-Enum-SB at R=100, sEH:

| pool | seed 43 | seed 44 | solver |
|---|---|---|---|
| 21k–27k (pre-cap-fix enum) | 37 | 23 | Optimal |
| 50,000 | 12 | 39 | ⚠️ TimeLimit |
| uncapped (123,298 / 149,604) | 27 | 25 | ⚠️ TimeLimit (43,080 s) |

Raising the cap 2 h → 12 h and relaxing `gapRel` to 1e-3 did not achieve convergence, and the capped
answers **do not trend** — seed 43 reads 12, 27, 37 across three pool sizes with no ordering — so they
are not approximately right; they are bounds at unknown distance. The converged rows all used the
pre-fix truncated enumerations, so they are not usable either. **The arm is reportable only as a lower
bound on the competitor, which flatters us, and cannot carry a ratio.** Our own selection returns an
answer on the same pool in ~1 s.

**5 — every failure in this entry was silent.** None raised; all returned a plausible number:

| failure | what it produced |
|---|---|
| empty `routes.json` | every hub skipped → SPARROW prices the EMPTY library as trivially `Optimal` at zero cost |
| no `children[].reaction` | each child's route stops at its hub → prices the hub, reports `Optimal` |
| wrong snapshot | recipe coverage reads **100%** while only **53%** of the run's fragments are expandable (549 of 1,169 bought rather than built) |
| enumeration rewritten mid-experiment | two arms priced on different candidate sets, both reporting success |
| solver hit its time limit | CBC returns `Optimal` regardless; detected by wall-clock, not status |

The common shape is an **existence check where the real question was a match or content check** — the
file was present, well-formed, and wrong. Each now aborts.

**6 — what published results depend on.** Nothing in the matrix headline (2.93× at R=100, 30/31 cells)
or the competitor headline (2.51×, within-seed, n=3) uses recipes or `routes.json`: both are priced by
count-once, which reads `{smiles, reward, added_promoted}` and never a route. The recipe gap costs the
dataset's breadth and one ablation's scope, not the main claim.
